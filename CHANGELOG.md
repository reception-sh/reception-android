# Changelog

All notable changes to the Reception Android SDK are documented here.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [1.0.0] — 2026-11-12

First public release, with the features of the Reception iOS SDK 1.7.0. Requires Android 7.0 (API 24) or later and an app built with `compileSdk` 35 or later; on Android 7.0 itself (not 7.1.1 and later) the chat reaches the Reception service only if your app's network security configuration adds Let's Encrypt's root certificates, which that version predates. Works with the Jetpack Compose your app uses, from Compose 1.7 (BOM 2024.12.01, which the SDK is built against) through Compose 1.12 (BOM 2026.09.00) and Material 3 1.5 (1.5.0-rc01), which every release is tested on. The SDK draws its chat sheet itself, so no Material 3 version your app resolves can break it.

### Added

- `Reception.configure(context, appId)` connects your app with its public App ID from the Reception dashboard. Install `sh.reception:reception` from Maven Central; no manifest edits, R8 rules or other dependencies are needed, also when your app shrinks with its own R8 configuration without Android's default ProGuard file.
- Native support chat with text and photos, opened with `Reception.openChat()` as a bottom sheet in Material 3's look over the current activity, or embedded as the `ReceptionChatView()` composable or the `ReceptionChatView` View. The sheet closes by drag, scrim tap and (predictive) Back; while the keyboard is open (docked or floating), a drag from its handle or title bar closes it and a drag over the messages puts the keyboard away; a touch catches the sheet while it slides. In landscape the sheet sits beside a side navigation bar or display cutout when it has room, and its content always stays clear of them. An embedded chat moves its composer only by the part of the keyboard and system bars that covers it, so it sits on the keyboard also above a tab bar or bottom navigation, in a container your app scales, in a Compose `Dialog` or a bottom sheet, and after your app moves the view into another window. In a Compose `Dialog` on Android 11 and later the SDK reads none of the dialog's flags: an edge-to-edge dialog (`decorFitsSystemWindows = false`, or Android 15 and later enforcing edge-to-edge) lifts the composer with the keyboard frame by frame, and a dialog that keeps the keyboard's inset to itself (Compose's default decor) is resized for the keyboard once the SDK sees that (checked again at every keyboard show, also a quick hide and show), so on its first keyboard show the composer reaches the keyboard about half a second after it (and on Android 16 with an app targeting 34, whenever Android misses the resize, a third of a second late). Known limit of Compose 1.7's `Dialog` (`DialogLayout`) with `usePlatformDefaultWidth = false`: it can lay the content out against the display instead of its window, so the dialog itself hides the chat's composer. Measured: on Android 11 and 14 a full-width dialog whose decor fits system windows (Compose's default) keeps the composer under the keyboard, while a full-width edge-to-edge one (`decorFitsSystemWindows = false`) works; on Android 16 both full-width variants hide the composer even without a keyboard. In a full-width dialog give the chat a bounded height, keep the platform's default width, or show the chat as a screen or with `Reception.openChat()`. An adjust mode your app sets on a window while the chat is in it (for example `adjustPan` on its dialog) stays: the SDK then leaves that window's mode alone and does not restore an older one over it. Before Android 11 a chat in a Compose `Dialog` moves with the dialog to keep the cursor in view, and the keyboard may cover the composer's lower edge. Tapping a photo opens it full screen over the status and navigation bars and the display cutout, with pinch zoom, drag to dismiss and Back, on every supported Android and Compose version.
- `Reception.unreadCount` and `Reception.isChatOpen` as `StateFlow`s for your own entry point and badge, plus `addUnreadCountListener` for Java code.
- `identify(userId, name, email)`, `setMetadata(_)`, `logout()` and `deleteData()` (suspending, or with a callback) for accounts, customer context, sign-out and account deletion.
- Identity verification with `identifyWithToken(token)` for tokens signed by your own server, and the `IdentityRejected` event.
- Push notifications for replies through Firebase Cloud Messaging: the SDK fetches and registers the FCM token by itself when your app includes Firebase Messaging and notifications are allowed, never asking for permission. `ReceptionMessagingService` receives the messages for apps without a messaging service of their own and registers a new token only while notifications are allowed; others forward them with `handlePushMessage(context, message.data, message.messageId)` and `setPushToken(token)`. Notifications use their own "Support replies" channel in the person's language and are not shown while the chat is open; a repeated FCM message shows once. Taps open the chat with no code in your app, also when the app was not running.
- Launcher badges follow the unread count while `updatesAppBadge` is on (the default); `notificationSmallIcon` sets your notification icon, which the SDK remembers for messages that start your app's process.
- A card in the chat can ask for notification permission on Android 13 and later after the person's message, when enabled in the dashboard, and a status line confirms that replies will arrive as notifications.
- Google Play review cards and paywall cards. Register `Reception.paywalls`, present your own paywall from `onPaywall`, and limit offers with `paywallAvailability`.
- Link cards that open websites, other apps, Google Play, mail and the phone without code; web links open in the app that claims them, otherwise in a Custom Tab over the chat (`opensWebLinksInApp = false` hands them to the browser). Links to your app's own intent filters close the chat first. `Reception.screens` lists the places in your app that links can open.
- `Reception.track(event, opensChat)` reports app events for automations. With Google Play Billing in your app, device registration reports whether the person has paid access, and the SDK reports `subscription_cancelled` and `subscription_expired`.
- Local appearance options with `ReceptionColor` for light and dark mode (one color, a pair, or a color resource with a night variant; `ReceptionColor.argb` for Java), a custom `FontFamily`, welcome icon and close button options, plus remote appearance, texts, team photos and names published from the dashboard. Device registration reports the appearance your app sets in code, so the dashboard's design editor starts from it.
- Built-in text in 48 languages, following the app's per-app or system language, with `languageOverride` for in-app language settings; font scale up to Android's largest, where the title bar grows with its title (one centered line that ends in an ellipsis when it is too long, never under the close button) and card buttons wrap their label between their icons, dark mode and right-to-left layouts.
- Events through `onEvent` for your analytics.
- Automatic retries for interrupted sends and rate-limit waits that keep drafts and resume on their own. The live connection reopens at once when the service renews a connection that stayed up at least 10 seconds, and backs off like any failure when it is asked for a new connection sooner, so a misbehaving server or proxy cannot make the chat flood the service; a connection opened in the last seconds of its hourly access token is renewed that soon too, rarely, and then reconnects after a short wait (the connection banner may show "Connecting" meanwhile, and no message is lost). The connection banner shows "Connecting" only when the live connection does not come up within a second of the chat listening, not while a chat that stays online is still opening, also not one an automation opened. Without a network it shows "No connection" at once, and when the network returns while the chat is still opening it shows "Connecting" at once, on purpose, until the chat is connected.
- An automation's message that is scheduled for later shows in a chat that is open on its welcome screen once the device learns of it: when the app comes to the foreground again, a push arrives or the chat is opened again. While the chat simply stays open and no push reaches the device, the chat does not ask for it, as on iOS.
- The chat sheet comes back after the system ended the app's process while it was on screen. In apps that call `configure` at startup (in `Application.onCreate`) Android restores it as it was, with its scroll position and an open photo. In apps that configure later, as React Native and Flutter apps do from JavaScript or Dart, your app's screen comes up at once and takes touches, and the chat opens again, already open, when your app calls `configure`, if that call, the SDK's check of the stored chat and a resumed screen of your app all happen within 5 seconds of the return (counted with the time the device sleeps) and nothing closed or opened the chat meanwhile; it then starts at the latest messages and an open photo is closed (the draft text is kept, photos picked but not sent are not). Until then your app's screen shows and takes touches. `closeChat()`, `logout()` and `deleteData()` cancel that return also when they are called before `configure` (they do nothing else then); call `closeChat()` when your app knows the chat must not come back. In React Native apps a deep link, shortcut or notification that reaches the app's running main activity meanwhile cancels it too; one that starts a new activity does not (a tap on a Reception notification opens the chat as usual, once). Otherwise, or later, it stays closed. In an app whose launcher activity is `singleTask` (as in Expo apps), returning through the app icon makes Android close every screen above that activity, the chat included; the chat comes back open when that activity is a `ComponentActivity` (an `AppCompatActivity`, a `FragmentActivity`, or React Native's `ReactActivity` with react-native-reception 1.6.0 or later, which hands the SDK the new intents React Native does not pass on; not Flutter's `FlutterActivity`), the launcher marks its start as Android's launchers do (`FLAG_ACTIVITY_RESET_TASK_IF_NEEDED`), Android closed the chat within a second of that start, and that activity is the next screen to appear, also after a rotation with the chat open or after Android recreated that activity from its saved state (unless Android saved that state before your app called `configure`). Otherwise Android's behaviour stays and the chat is closed: after your notifications, deep links, shortcuts, widgets or your own navigation with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_CLEAR_TOP`, with a launcher that does not mark its start that way, or with a launcher activity that is not a `ComponentActivity`. An intent your app or a library builds itself with the launcher's action, category and that flag counts as a return through the icon.
- Setup check on debuggable builds: launching the app with the extra `ReceptionSetupCheck` sends one message and logs whether it was delivered.
- The SDK works in your app's main process. If your app runs code in other processes (for example `Application.onCreate` in a process of a service with `android:process`, or an isolated service), `configure` there logs an error and does nothing, and a messaging service that forwards Reception messages must run in the main process.
- Device sessions with short-lived access and credentials encrypted with a key in the Android Keystore, excluded from backups.
